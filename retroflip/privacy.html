RetroFlip

Privacy policy

Last changed 3 October 2026

RetroFlip is an Android app that turns a phone on a bedside table into a flip-clock dock. I, Pablo Laso, wrote it for my own phone; it isn't sold or offered in any app store. It has no server of its own, so nothing it handles is ever sent to me or to anyone acting for me.

Your Google account

RetroFlip only touches a Google account if you switch its security camera on and sign in with Google. It then asks for three permissions, and uses each for one thing:

  • Its own files in your Drive (drive.file). It makes a folder called "RetroFlip camera", puts the camera's clips and snapshots in it, one folder per event, and deletes each event for good, skipping the bin, within 30 hours. This permission only reaches files RetroFlip made itself; the rest of your Drive stays out of its sight.
  • Sending email as you (gmail.send). One alert per event, from your address to your address, with a snapshot, the first clip and a link to the event's folder, plus a test alert when you ask for one. It can't read, search or delete any of your mail.
  • Your email address (userinfo.email). To show which account is signed in, and to address the alerts.

All of it goes straight from the phone to Google, inside your own account. The phone keeps your email address in RetroFlip's settings; the access itself is held by Google Play services on the phone, never by RetroFlip on a server. None of it is shared, sold, used for advertising or used for anything beyond the above.

RetroFlip's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The camera

The camera opens only while it's armed, which is while your iPhone is away from the home Wi-Fi. It records video, never sound. To tell whether the iPhone is home, the dock looks for it on the home network; that check never leaves the network. Clips wait in the app's private storage until they're uploaded, and are deleted from the phone then, or within 30 hours if they never go. A clip still recording when the iPhone comes back is thrown away unseen.

The optional live view is a password-protected page on the dock that shows frames only while the camera is armed. It answers only through Tailscale, between your own devices, encrypted, and never on the Wi-Fi itself.

What else leaves the phone

  • The forecast and air quality: the phone's position, rounded to about a kilometre, to Open-Meteo, and for weather warnings to the US National Weather Service.
  • Travel times, if switched on: an event's address to Android's geocoder, which is Google's, and the rounded position and the event's place to routing.openstreetmap.de.

That's all. The voice is made on the phone, and the calendar, photos, sleep sounds and what's playing are read there and never sent anywhere. There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no account to make.

Stopping it

In RetroFlip's settings, under CAMERA, SIGN OUT stops anything more going to Google, and switching the camera off stops the camera. To withdraw RetroFlip's access to your account completely, remove it at myaccount.google.com/connections. RetroFlip deletes what it put in your Drive only while it's signed in, so after signing out, delete the "RetroFlip camera" folder yourself if anything is left in it.
